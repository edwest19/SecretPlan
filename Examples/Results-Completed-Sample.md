# Medicare Part D Prescription Plan Analysis

## Executive Summary

This analysis provides a comprehensive Medicare Part D prescription plan evaluation for a 72-year-old resident of Suffolk County, NY (ZIP code 11743) for the 2027 plan year.

The applicant is currently taking five medications (three low-cost generics and two high-cost brand-name drugs: **Eliquis** and **Jardiance**). Under the Inflation Reduction Act redesign for 2027, the standard annual Medicare Part D maximum out-of-pocket (MOOP) threshold is capped at **$2,400**. Because of the two brand-name medications, the applicant will reach this cap early in the year under almost every available Part D plan.

**Provisional Key Findings:**

* **Recommended Plan:** Stand-alone Part D plans like the **Wellcare Value Script (PDP)** or similar benchmark/low-premium plans provide the lowest overall annual out-of-pocket exposure.
* **Cost Structure:** With high-cost brand-name drugs, total annual prescription drug expenses will hit the **$2,400 out-of-pocket cap**, plus the plan's monthly premiums and deductible.
* **New York EPIC Eligibility:** Because the applicant’s income ($50,000–$75,000) falls within the qualifying threshold (up to $75,000 for single residents), the applicant qualifies for the **NY EPIC Deductible Plan**. This provides secondary coverage and a Special Enrollment Period (SEP).

## Answers to Special Questions

1. **Are any current medications subject to restrictions?**
* **Eliquis** and **Jardiance** are subject to **Quantity Limits (QL)** (e.g., 60 tablets per 30 days for Eliquis; 30 tablets per 30 days for Jardiance). Neither typically requires Step Therapy or Prior Authorization for standard FDA-approved indications on major formularies.


2. **Does Costco provide preferred-network pricing under recommended plans?**
* **Yes.** Costco is a preferred retail pharmacy under the **Wellcare Value Script** and **Humana** standalone Part D plans, offering lower copays during the initial coverage phase.


3. **Does mail order provide meaningful savings?**
* In 2027, with high-cost brand-name medications pushing the applicant to the $2,400 cap early in the year, mail order provides limited *financial* savings. However, it offers significant **convenience** for 90-day maintenance supplies, especially given the applicant's travel within New York State.


4. **Which plans provide the best protection if a new expensive medication is prescribed?**
* Due to the strict **$2,400 Out-of-Pocket Cap** enforced under federal law in 2027, **all Part D plans provide equal financial protection** against extreme spending. Once total out-of-pocket spending reaches $2,400, the plan pays 100% of all covered formulary drugs for the rest of the year. Plans with broader overall formularies (such as UHC/AARP or Humana) provide slightly higher flexibility if new brand-name medications require coverage without formulary exceptions.


5. **Does New York EPIC appear relevant for this applicant's situation?**
* **Yes, absolutely.**
* **Eligibility:** With an income between $50,000 and $75,000 (single applicant limit is $75,000), the applicant qualifies for the **EPIC Deductible Plan**.
* **Benefits:** EPIC acts as a secondary payer after Medicare Part D. Additionally, enrolling in EPIC grants a Special Enrollment Period (SEP), allowing the applicant to switch Part D plans once per year outside the standard Open Enrollment Period if needed.


6. **What assumptions would most likely change the recommendation?**
* If a generic version of Eliquis becomes widely available and placed on Tier 1/2, overall drug costs prior to reaching the cap would drop significantly.
* If the applicant changes primary pharmacy preferences away from preferred partners (e.g., refusing to use Costco or Walgreens under Wellcare).
